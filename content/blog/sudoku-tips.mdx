The difference between a beginner who finishes an easy puzzle in five minutes and one who stares at the grid for an hour is rarely talent. It is a handful of habits. These ten sudoku tips are practical, not philosophical: they each change something concrete about how you look at the grid, and they stack. Skip the ones that do not click and come back later.

## 1. Scan one digit at a time

Beginners scan "the next empty cell." Better players scan "where can a specific digit go?" Pick a digit — say 4 — and walk every row, column, and box that could accept it. Do this for each of the nine digits before you ever pick up your pencil. This is the single fastest way to surface moves most people miss, and it turns scanning from a random search into a checklist.

## 2. Start with naked singles

A **naked single** is a cell whose every possible digit is ruled out except one. When you find one, fill it and let the ripples hit the board. Naked singles appear constantly on easy and medium puzzles, and each one you place narrows the board for everything else. Train yourself to see them first — not the flashy patterns.

## 3. Write pencil notes like your memory depends on it

Your working memory holds maybe a few digits at a time. A 9x9 grid has dozens of live possibilities. Pencil notes are the difference between forever re-scanning and actually advancing. On the [play page](/play/) toggle pencil mode and jot candidates in any cell that is not obvious. Cluttered notes feel slower at first; they are dramatically faster in aggregate.

## 4. Look for hidden singles before you brute-force

A **hidden single** is a digit that has one legal home in a row, column, or box, even though that cell has other candidates. Scan each unit and ask: "of this digit's possible spots, how many remain?" When the answer is one, write it in, then clear that cell's other candidates. Hidden singles are the engine behind most medium difficulty solves.

## 5. Anchor on givens when you are stuck

When nothing moves, stop solving the empty grid and re-read the numbers already on the board. Every given is a constraint screaming at you. Pick one and trace all three directions it blocks — row, column, box — and watch what candidates fall away. Most "no next move" moments dissolve once you re-orient on the givens instead of the empties.

## 6. Never guess on a well-formed puzzle

A proper sudoku has exactly one solution, solvable by logic. If you catch yourself guessing a digit "to see what happens," you have either hit a puzzle harder than your current techniques or you made an earlier mistake. Flip to the [solver](/solver/), load your current board, and let it show you the next logical step. Guessing is not a shortcut; it is a signal.

## 7. Fix small mistakes as soon as you spot them

A single wrong digit placed early can turn the last third of a puzzle into a brick wall. If the end of a puzzle suddenly feels impossible, your instinct should be "where did I go wrong," not "maybe it is just hard." Scan numbers you placed yourself (not givens) against their row, column, and box. The conflict is usually within one move of where things seized up.

## 8. Build a scan routine and repeat it

Great solvers are not smarter; they are methodical. Build a fixed loop: scan all nine digits for hidden singles, fill every naked single, check your notes, repeat. Run the loop until it produces nothing, then re-read the givens, then run it again. Routine beats inspiration, every single time.

## 9. Use the tools you built

There is no shame in leaning on good tools when a puzzle runs long. The [printable page](/printable/) gives you clean grids to work on paper when screens get distracting. The [solver](/solver/) explains the next step when you are genuinely stuck. Both exist to keep you solving and learning, not to do the work for you.

## 10. Learn one advanced technique at a time

Do not try to learn every sudoku technique in one sitting. Pick one — say, naked pairs, pointing pairs, or the [x-wing](/blog/x-wing-sudoku) — and play easy puzzles deliberately hunting for it. Once you can spot it without thinking, add the next. Techniques compound, but only if you actually use the ones you have.

## Put the tips together

Starting your next puzzle, run the single-digit scan first, fill the naked singles, write notes in anything uncertain, and hunt hidden singles. [Unlock harder puzzles and build the habit](/play/). If a wall appears, open the [solver](/solver/) and read its reasoning. Ten small habits, done consistently, are the whole difference between someone who plays sudoku and someone who solves it.
