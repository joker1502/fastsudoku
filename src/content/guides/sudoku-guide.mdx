Sudoku looks intimidating at first — a 9x9 grid full of numbers — but the rules are simple enough to learn in two minutes. This guide walks you through everything you need to start solving your first puzzle today.

## The Rules in 30 Seconds

- Fill the grid so every **row**, **column**, and **3x3 box** contains the digits **1 through 9** exactly once.
- Some numbers are already filled in. These are your **clues** — you never change them.
- There is exactly **one correct solution** to every properly made puzzle.

That's it. No math, no addition, no language barrier. Sudoku is pure logic.

## Reading the Grid

A standard sudoku has:

- **9 rows** (numbered 1–9 from top to bottom)
- **9 columns** (numbered 1–9 from left to right)
- **9 boxes** (3x3 regions, sometimes drawn with thicker lines)

Every cell belongs to exactly one row, one column, and one box. A number can only appear **once** in each of those three groups. That single rule drives every solving technique there is.

## Your First Technique: Scanning

Start by scanning the grid for rows, columns, or boxes that are nearly full. The fewer empty cells a group has, the easier it is to fill.

**Example:** if a row has 7 numbers already, only 2 digits are missing. Check which of those 2 digits already appear in the intersecting columns and boxes — often only one spot remains valid.

This process is called **scanning**, and it solves most easy puzzles on its own.

## The Second Technique: Single Candidate

Pick an empty cell and ask: which numbers are **not** already in its row, column, or box? If only one number is possible, write it down. This is called a **single candidate** (or *naked single*).

Work through the grid cell by cell with this method, and easy and medium puzzles practically solve themselves.
